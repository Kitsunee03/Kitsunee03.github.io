## Project overview

Junkonaut is built around a simple physical loop: collect space junk, feed it to the Glorious Hole, and use the resulting progression to improve the tools that make the next cleanup run faster and more satisfying.

The project started in my third year as an annual assignment intended to become a vertical slice in roughly five months. After evaluating its potential and the work completed by No Name Yeti, ENTI-UB allowed us to continue development during our fourth year. We are now using the project as the foundation for our final degree projects, building on the previous year's work while pushing it towards a polished, commercially viable release.

Our team covers programming, design, art and music, with FMOD integrated into the audio workflow. This year, one of our musicians stayed on to complete their final degree project with us, and a classmate joined as Producer. Her work focuses on that role, helping us turn ideas into concrete priorities and set feasible, realistic goals.

## My role

The developers rotate between features depending on availability and affinity with each subject. My work has focused mainly on the Grapple Gun, gravity refinement, NPC dialogue and movement, missions, and the inventory and equipment upgrade systems. The latter use DataTables as an internal database for defining and managing the available content.

## The Grapple Gun

The Grapple Gun has been the most difficult part of my contributions and the feature with the greatest impact on exploration. Unreal Engine's Rope Component presented limitations and stability problems that frequently broke immersion, so we decided to build our own rope system.

<details>
<summary>How the custom rope system works</summary>

After many iterations, the system dynamically generates intermediate points that behave as rope folds. Raycasts detect obstacles and create a new point when the rope needs to bend around the environment. Once a point exists, the system checks whether it is still necessary by tracing directly from point A to point C. If that path is clear, the intermediate point B is removed and the iteration stops, limiting changes to a single fold per tick. This keeps only the points required by the current geometry.

The system checks the direction of each segment between points A and B and applies the corresponding force to the player, allowing the rope to directly influence their movement. It also tracks a maximum rope length, updating it as segments are created or removed. When the player exceeds that length, a traction force pulls them back within the constraint.

Movement takes the nearest fold into account, allowing the player to pivot around obstacles and creating behaviour inspired by the response of a real rope.

</details>

## What comes next

The system still needs significant optimisation and refinement, but its complexity and importance within the game make it a likely focus for my final degree project. I hope to explore collision detection, dynamic point management and the player's physical response in greater depth while iterating towards a robust, consistent and polished result.

The current build focuses on the core interaction loop, the upgrade curve, and the strange characters encountered along the way. The Grapple Gun is especially important to the way Junkonaut's exploration feels: it is both a traversal tool and an ongoing technical experiment in making a custom rope system behave consistently in a changing environment.
